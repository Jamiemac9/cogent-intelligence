Half of UK small businesses now use AI. Here's where agents beat chat tools

Almost half of UK small business owners now use AI, more than double last year. Most use it to write and think. Here's where a chat tool is all you need, and where an agent starts to pay for itself.

On this page

  1. The short answer
  2. What the survey found
  3. When a chat tool is enough
  4. Where an agent earns its keep
  5. The worries are the right ones
  6. A sensible first step

01The short answer

47% of UK small business owners now use AI tools, up from 22% in 2025, according to Simply Business's 2026 SME Insights Report. Most of that use is a chat tool helping one person write, research or think. An AI agent is worth it when the same jobs repeat every week, need doing when nobody is at a keyboard, and run across your inbox, diary and accounts.

Key idea

A chat tool makes one person faster. An agent takes a repeating job off the whole business.

02What did the survey find?

Simply Business surveyed 1,842 UK small business owners between 30 July and 7 August 2026 and published the results on 8 September 2026 (Simply Business, small business AI usage doubles, checked 12 November 2026). Among owners already using AI, these were the main uses:

What owners use AI forShare of AI users
Content creation63%
Problem solving53%
Generating ideas50%
Saving time on admin46%

Confidence is lower than adoption. Only 19% of owners called themselves very confident using AI day to day, and 31% said they don't understand how to use it. A further 13% plan to start within the next six to 12 months.

03When is a chat tool enough?

A chat tool such as ChatGPT or Claude is the right answer when the work is one-off and someone is there to do it. Writing a newsletter, rewording a tricky email, thinking through a pricing change: a personal subscription handles those well and costs little.

The top three uses in the survey (content, problem solving and ideas) all fit that pattern. If that's where your time goes, you probably don't need an agent yet, and we'd say so on a call. Our plain-English comparison of AI agents and ChatGPT goes through the difference in more detail.

04Where does an agent earn its keep?

The fourth use, saving time on admin, is where chat tools run out. Admin repeats, it starts when something happens rather than when you open an app, and it lives across several tools. A chat tool can draft the chaser email. Someone still has to spot the overdue invoice, paste the text, send it and check for a reply.

An agent does the whole job inside the tools you already use, on a trigger or a schedule, and logs what it did. The jobs where that pays off tend to share three traits:

  • They repeat every week, such as enquiry replies, booking confirmations, invoice chasers or document requests.
  • They happen out of hours, like the enquiry that lands late at night or the timesheet that didn't arrive.
  • They cross systems, from inbox to calendar to accounts to CRM.

To find yours, our find your peak method scores each job by hours lost and how easy it is to hand over. The highest score is usually the first agent.

05Are the worries about AI justified?

Yes, and they're the right ones. Owners not using AI gave three main reasons in the same survey: security and privacy concerns (44%), no clear use for it (39%) and worries about accuracy (36%).

Each has a practical answer. On privacy, the ICO's guidance on AI and data protection sets out how it applies the law to AI systems that handle personal data, and it's the place to start before any tool reads customer details (checked 12 November 2026). On accuracy, the fix is approval rather than blind trust: anything that goes to a customer or touches money waits for a person to say yes. We explain how that works in approvals first.

On "no clear use", that's often true, and it's the honest reason to stay with a chat tool for now.

06What's a sensible first step?

Write down the five jobs that ate most of your time last week. If none of them repeat, keep using a chat tool and revisit in six months. If two or three repeat every week, that's where one agent can start.
